MacRumors (Counterpoint)

Why it matters: the trade-secrets fight is now two-way and reaches into the AI-device race — OpenAI’s first hardware device, reportedly due in 2027, would compete directly with the iPhone maker, though every claim on both sides remains an allegation and trial is set for July 19, 2027.   • 2026-11-25 — Research firm SemiAnalysis reported that Amazon’s Trainium3 UltraServer delivered up to 2.28 times better performance per dollar than Nvidia’s GB300 NVL72 on its AgentX agentic-inference benchmark — Amazon’s first Trainium3 submission — with the edge coming mostly from modeled ownership cost ($1.05 per chip-hour vs $2.31 for the GB300) rather than raw throughput, where the lead was about 4% at the highlighted operating point; SemiAnalysis separately found Nvidia’s newer Vera Rubin rack beats the GB300 by 2–6.5 times on the same measure. Why it matters: the first independent benchmark showing AWS’s custom silicon is cost-competitive in AI inference — a margin and positioning story for AWS — though the figure is a preview maximum on modeled costs against last-generation Blackwell hardware, not a full head-to-head. TIKR (SemiAnalysis)

ASML — ASML Holding

  • 2026-11-26 — Dutch Trade Minister Sjoerd Sjoerdsma told parliament on Thursday that the U.S. MATCH Act goes “way too far” and would put Europe at an unacceptable disadvantage, saying the Netherlands is lobbying hard against the bill together with the European Commission, Germany and France to keep it out of the annual defence policy bill, and that if it is adopted the Netherlands will tell the U.S. administration not to enforce it; the bill would force allies including the Netherlands and Japan to align DUV export controls with U.S. levels or face unilateral U.S. restrictions on equipment including ASML’s immersion DUV tools. Why it matters: ministerial-level Dutch opposition, now coordinated across the EU, is the strongest defense yet of ASML’s China business — about 20% of this year’s sales — though the bill could still advance in the defense bill.   • 2026-11-25 — ASML chief executive Christophe Fouquet warned on Semafor’s Future Proof programme that cutting China off completely from chip tools would backfire, saying export controls are needed to protect very advanced technology but should not be so strict that they push China to escalate its own development — countries should not “completely frustrate a country and invite that country to basically double down or triple down on development”; ASML has held an extremely high market share in China for almost 30 years. Why it matters: the ASML chief is now publicly making the case against a total ban as Congress debates attaching the MATCH Act to the defence bill — China is about 20% of ASML’s sales, and a total ban would hit DUV tool sales and servicing while accelerating Chinese domestic rivals. CASRAI (Semafor, Nov 25)
  • 2026-11-27 — Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten said he will discuss the U.S. MATCH Act with President Donald Trump at the G20 summit in Miami on December 14–15, saying it is in both countries’ interests to find “a sensible way forward”; the Netherlands opposes the bill on sovereignty grounds, arguing that restricting ASML’s access to its global market would only spur innovation in China, and Jetten said the recent Nvidia affair “has triggered a shift in sentiment in the US Congress”. Why it matters: the dispute over ASML’s China business — about 20% of this year’s sales — has now reached the prime-minister-to-president level, the strongest diplomatic defence yet, though the bill could still advance in the defence bill. Reuters
