UNP — Union Pacific

  • 2026-11-19 — A coalition of 21 state attorneys general, led by Pennsylvania’s Dave Sunday, and joint shipper associations (chemical, fertilizer and petroleum groups) filed comments with the Surface Transportation Board urging it to reject the Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger, arguing its harms outweigh its benefits and that no conditions can sufficiently mitigate them; Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern filed a response opposing Canadian Pacific Kansas City’s request to end the review, and CEO Jim Vena apologized for his March remarks about raising rates on merger opponents. Why it matters: organised state and shipper opposition at the Nov 18 comment deadline raises regulatory risk for the transformational merger; DOJ/DOT comments are due Dec 3, replies Feb 16, 2027, and a decision is expected by Aug 2027. PA Attorney General
  • 2026-11-20 — The Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers and Trainmen won a $98.8 million arbitration award against Union Pacific: a Special Board of Adjustment ruled the railroad failed to implement a negotiated “11/4” consistent-scheduling work rule (11 days on, 4 off) for the period June 30, 2024 to January 27, 2025, and Union Pacific must calculate the payments owed within 30 days. Why it matters: a roughly $99 million one-off labour cost and a sign of strained union relations as the Norfolk Southern merger review proceeds; the award covers a benefit period that ended in January 2025. Reuters
  • 2026-11-20 — Four rail labor organizations — the SMART Transportation Division, the National Conference of Firemen and Oilers, the International Brotherhood of Boilermakers and SMART’s Mechanical Division — filed joint comments urging the Surface Transportation Board to approve the Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger, each having secured “Jobs for Life” employment agreements with the railroads; Canadian National separately filed proposed conditions with the STB aimed at preserving competition in Midwest markets. Why it matters: organised labour support with binding job guarantees is the strongest pro-merger voice yet in the review, countering the political and shipper opposition filed by the Nov 18 deadline; a decision is not expected before the second half of 2027.
