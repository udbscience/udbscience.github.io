ZBRA — Zebra Technologies

  • 2026-11-18 — Chief financial officer Nathan Winters said at the Stephens Annual Investment Conference in Nashville that Zebra expects 2027 sales growth at the high end of its typical organic range, supported by strong backlog and large last-mile delivery-device refreshes deploying through 2027 into 2028; memory-cost pressures are being offset by price increases and inventory build, with memory supply now secured from new suppliers; AI-driven productivity is expected to deliver about $100 million (a 150-basis-point opex improvement) this year with a similar amount next year; Zebra expects nearly $1 billion of free cash flow this year and has about $400 million left under its buyback authorization. Why it matters: the first quantified 2027 framework, given earlier than usual, confirms pricing power against the memory-cost headwind flagged in Q3 and updates capital return — directly relevant to 2027 earnings and valuation. MarketBeat, via TradingView ad-hoc-news, citing Investing.com

The refinancing of the $1.95 billion facility (Term Loan A plus revolver, due May 25, 2027) planned for later in November has still not been announced as of November 23. Zebra released its 2025 sustainability report on November 20 (Scope 1+2 emissions −42% versus 2020) — a routine ESG update with no financial impact disclosed, excluded. Zebra reported Q3 on November 3, outside this window (net sales $1,614 million, +22.3%; non-GAAP EPS $4.95, above consensus; full-year outlook raised; Q4 EPS guided to $5.00–$5.40), with management flagging rising memory-chip costs as a ~$145 million full-year headwind and guiding 2027 margins below 2026 levels. The company carries $2.58 billion of debt against $182 million of cash, with $1.95 billion now reclassified as current liabilities.
