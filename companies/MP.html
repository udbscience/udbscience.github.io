MP — MP Materials

  • 2026-11-05 — MP Materials reported Q3 2026 results after the close: revenue $147.7M (+176% year over year) vs about $117M consensus, adjusted EPS +$0.04 vs an expected about $0.02 loss, and adjusted EBITDA $41.1M vs -$12.6M a year ago; NdPr production was 1,018 MT (+41%) and sales rose 94%, PPA income was $8.1M, and GM magnet qualification is complete with saleable deliveries starting in Q4; the GAAP loss was -$0.12 per share. Why it matters: a clear beat on the preview benchmarks that turns adjusted earnings positive, with the magnet business reaching its first commercial deliveries — the key test of the speculative case — though GAAP remains loss-making and the stock closed at a new low before the print.   • 2026-11-09 — MP Materials CFO Ryan Corbett said at the D.A. Davidson Critical Minerals Conference that MP expects to produce 5,000–6,000 metric tons of NdPr in 2027, with production costs falling as output rises and the first train of its chlor-alkali facility comes online early in 2027; he said magnet deliveries after GM qualification will be 'quite modest' for the next couple of quarters as customers introduce magnets across products and plants. Why it matters: the first 2027 production range since the Q3 print, framing the ramp and the cost path for the magnet business, while tempering near-term magnet revenue expectations. The Markets Daily
  • 2026-11-11 — CEO Jim Litinsky said at the Baird Global Industrial Conference that MP has qualified rare-earth magnets for General Motors and is ramping production, plans to bring Apple online as a customer next year — Apple has agreed to buy at least $500 million of magnets from the Independence facility — and that the Pentagon has contracted for 100% of the planned 10X magnet production; he said the rare-earth oxide run rate is above 50,000 tons and moving toward 60,000 tons, and that robotics magnet inquiries already exceed planned 10X output. Why it matters: the first detail on the magnet customer ramp since the Q3 print — GM qualified, Apple next, Pentagon offtake locked — the core of the speculative case, though large-scale robotics demand is still years out.
