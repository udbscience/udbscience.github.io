ARM — Arm Holdings

  • 2026-11-17 — The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule (November 15) requiring licences for advanced-chip shipments to data centres in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil and curbing remote access to advanced compute by China-headquartered firms; the rule, published in the Federal Register on November 19, constrains where advanced AI chips can be deployed. Why it matters: it directly touches Arm's data-centre royalty runway behind its raised AGI CPU outlook, since it limits the markets where advanced AI accelerators — and the Arm-based CPUs paired with them — can be sold or accessed remotely. Kalkine
  • 2026-11-18 — Bloomberg reported that Heidrick & Struggles, the executive-search firm hired by Nvidia's board, has formally approached Arm chief executive Rene Haas about succeeding Jensen Huang, stepping up the search after the Financial Times first reported Nvidia's interest on November 13; no offer has been reported, and Nvidia board chair Harvey Jones said on the November 17 earnings call that the board “does not intend to reinstate” Huang. Why it matters: the search firm's formal approach makes the threat of losing the chief executive who led Arm's 2023 listing and its push into own-silicon (AGI CPUs) more concrete than the initial report — though no offer has been reported and the firm is approaching other candidates too. Inc., reporting Bloomberg Bloomberg Law
  • 2026-11-23 — Arm announced that chief executive Rene Haas has signed a new agreement to lead the company through 2033 and will also become vice chairman of SoftBank Group, its majority owner, effective December 1, 2026; Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive the same day, ending the succession search that had put Haas in the frame, and Bloomberg reported Haas had rebuffed the approaches. Why it matters: removes the leadership-overhang risk that had shadowed Arm since Huang's resignation — the chief executive who led the 2023 listing and the push into own-silicon (AGI CPUs) is now locked in through 2033, with a bigger SoftBank role on top. Reuters

Arm reported fiscal Q2 2027 on November 4, outside this window (revenue $1.44 billion, +27%; non-GAAP EPS $0.52; licensing +50% but royalties only +8%; Q3 guide revenue $1.54 billion, EPS $0.56; management sees up to $2 billion of AGI CPU revenue across fiscal 2027–2028; shares fell about 13% on November 5). At a November 11 fireside chat Arm reiterated AGI CPU customer orders of just under $2 billion across fiscal 2027–2028, with first commercial deliveries expected in March — a restatement of the November 4 disclosure, not new news. A November 19 recap of the November 4 call disclosed Meta as the principal AGI CPU customer, with first-generation gross margins of 30–40% (longer-term target above 50%). November 20 coverage of the Haas approach was follow-on commentary, with no offer reported and Arm declining to comment. Arm shares rose about 16% on November 23 on the Haas announcement, in the broad AI rebound after Nvidia reinstated Huang. Arm launched the Cortex-M42 processor on November 10, outside this window, and added it to its Flexible Access and Total Access licensing portfolios. Haas called Huang “a legendary figure” at an Austin tech conference on November 18.
