AAPL — Apple

  • 2026-11-22 — Apple agreed to a $250 million class-action settlement over claims it advertised Apple Intelligence Siri features for iPhones that were not delivered as promised; eligible U.S. buyers of iPhone 15 Pro/Pro Max and iPhone 16 models (purchased Jun 10, 2024–Mar 29, 2025) can claim $25–$95 per device, with a claims deadline of Dec 21, 2026 and a final approval hearing Feb 24, 2027; Apple denies wrongdoing. Why it matters: closes a reputational and legal overhang on the Apple Intelligence rollout, though $250 million is small next to Apple and court approval is still pending. The Mac Observer (via Quantli)
  • 2026-11-23 — U.S. startup GG Technologies sued Apple in India over the NameDrop contact-sharing feature, seeking about 15 billion rupees (~$162 million) in damages and a ban on sales and imports of iPhones and Apple Watches that include NameDrop; no injunction has been granted. Why it matters: a sales-ban request in a major growth market, though it is an early-stage claim by a small plaintiff and no injunction has been granted. MacRumors
  • 2026-11-23 — Thirty-two states and the District of Columbia filed a friend-of-the-court brief at the U.S. Supreme Court urging the justices to reject Apple’s bid to overturn a 2025 contempt order over its App Store, arguing a ruling for Apple “would be a windfall for competition-law violators”; the justices hear the appeal on Jan 11, 2027. Why it matters: the states’ backing strengthens Epic’s position in the App Store antitrust fight that governs Apple’s services economics, though the contempt order itself does not change the App Store rules already in force. Reuters
