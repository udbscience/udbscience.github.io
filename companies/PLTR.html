PLTR — Palantir

  • 2026-11-18 — A federal grand jury indicted Russian national Igor D'iu on charges of acting as a foreign agent to surveil Palantir employees — photographing computer screens near its Palo Alto offices and following staff — tied to the company's assistance to Ukraine; the Justice Department's papers name only “a Silicon Valley technology and defense company”, with Reuters (a person familiar with the investigation) and Bloomberg identifying it as Palantir. Why it matters: a state-sponsored targeting campaign against Palantir staff underscores the security and reputational exposure that comes with its Ukraine defence work, though it is not a charge against the company. Reuters

The November 16 items are now outside this window: UK ministers decided to end Palantir's NHS England Federated Data Platform contract in March 2029 (a two-year extension instead of exercising the break clause, with a new competitive procurement starting in the second half of 2027 on terms favouring UK suppliers), and Reuters reported European militaries actively seeking homegrown alternatives to Palantir (Germany's Helsing Clarity platform, France's Arcadia, Germany's AI data-integration shortlist excluding Palantir, the Netherlands wanting a European alternative within two years). D'iu is scheduled in court on November 24. Nvidia's third-quarter release on November 17 said it has partnered with Palantir to bring sovereign AI intelligence and Nvidia Nemotron open models to critical supply chains, and Palantir was named on the earnings call among companies rolling out offerings built on post-trained open models. On November 19 the Office of Government Ethics released President Trump's periodic transaction report showing his accounts bought Palantir shares on September 2 ($15,001–$50,000) and September 9 ($1,001–$15,000), ahead of two federal contracts totalling about $42.5 million (HUD $10.4 million initial obligations; DHS $32.1 million on September 29) — a conflict-of-interest optics story, though the amounts are tiny and CNBC found no evidence Trump directed the trades or that managers had advance knowledge. Also on November 19, OLIGO Security achieved FedRAMP Class D (High) certification for its runtime security platform through Palantir's FedStart program, letting federal agencies use it in their most sensitive environments — validating FedStart as a federal-ecosystem on-ramp, though no revenue or contract value for Palantir was disclosed. November 19 coverage of the NHS decision was re-reporting of the November 16 announcement, including the Drystone Health AI Collaboration consortium bidding for the successor contract. NHS England deleted the claim that 137 hospital trusts were live with and benefiting from Palantir's Federated Data Platform on November 12, after freedom-of-information research found 29 trusts were not using it or barely using it; the statistics regulator is examining the usage claims. The Guardian reported on November 9, outside this window, from messages released under freedom of information, that Palantir's UK chief lobbied a health minister to extend its NHS tools into GP patient records, which were expressly excluded from its £330 million contract. Guardian Australia reported on November 16, from freedom-of-information documents, that at least 10 Australian federal agencies share sensitive personal data with the Australian Criminal Intelligence Commission's Fraud Fusion Centre, which uses Palantir software as one of several analytics tools; the documents do not show any specific dataset was entered into Palantir, and the Australian National Audit Office is assessing the taskforce, with a report due in March 2027. Palantir won a £10.1 million five-year contract with the combined Bedfordshire, Cambridgeshire and Hertfordshire police forces for its BCH Foundry Platform, running October 9, 2026 to October 8, 2031 — its largest known UK police contract — reported November 13. Tampa General Hospital said on November 12 it is partnering with Palantir on analytical capabilities for its patient-safety platform. Former Democratic Senator Jon Tester joined Palantir as an adviser on its government business on November 9 (advisory, not lobbying). The Bureau of Investigative Journalism reported on November 10 that former Metropolitan Police commissioner Dame Cressida Dick has been a paid Palantir adviser since September 2025. The UK government decided on November 5, outside this window, not to trigger the break clause on Palantir's £330 million NHS Federated Data Platform contract, keeping the seven-year deal in place. Westpac said November 5 it will not renew its Palantir contract (financial-markets compliance and cyber security). AmeriLife announced an enterprise-wide Foundry and AIP agreement on November 5, with workflows already in production at Crump Life Insurance Services. The White House nominated Palantir chief information security officer Michael LoSapio to lead the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency on November 9; Senator Ron Wyden's office said the same week that compulsory processes are on the table if Palantir does not answer his questions next year as Finance Committee chairman.
