DIS — Walt Disney

  • 2026-11-21 — Members of Walt Disney World's Craft Maintenance Council voted down Disney's second contract offer, 231 yes to 735 no, after voting November 20–21; the unions say bargaining continues, with no strike authorized and no park operating change announced — the council's first offer was unanimously voted down on October 13, and its contract expired September 28 and is extended month to month. Why it matters: prolongs labor uncertainty for the Disney World maintenance workforce and keeps strike risk alive into December bargaining, though no walkout is scheduled and the Experiences segment — the earnings driver — is unaffected for now. Southern Regional Council of Carpenters
  • 2026-11-23 — Orlando Weekly reported that the Southern Regional Council of Carpenters filed an unfair labor practice charge with the National Labor Relations Board (case 12-CA-399130) alleging Disney managers told maintenance workers they were “replaceable” if they struck, showing a PowerPoint to that effect before the contract vote; the charge itself was filed on November 12 and Disney did not respond to requests for comment. Why it matters: escalates the Disney World maintenance dispute into a federal labor proceeding and keeps strike leverage alive into December bargaining, though the charge is unproven and no strike is called. Pirates & Princesses, reporting Orlando Weekly
  • 2026-11-23 — Disney said Brent A. Woodford, executive vice president for controllership, financial planning and tax and its principal accounting officer, notified the company of his retirement after 23 years; he stays as principal accounting officer through February 14, 2027 and as an employee through June 1, 2027, and David L. Haskett, senior controller for corporate controllership, succeeds him as principal accounting officer on February 15, 2027. Why it matters: a planned, orderly handover of financial-reporting leadership to an internal successor, so no disruption is signalled — a routine leadership change, not a governance event. WDW News Today

Disney reported fiscal Q4 2026 on November 17, just outside this window: adjusted EPS of $1.77 versus about $1.67 consensus, revenue of $25.9 billion (+16% versus about $25.2 billion expected), and total segment operating income of $5.0 billion (+43%); Experiences revenue was $10.1 billion (+15%, a record quarter and full year) and streaming SVOD operating income doubled to $824 million; the board raised the annual dividend 33% to $2.00 per share (two $1 installments, January 22 and July 13, 2027), guided cash content spending of about $26 billion in fiscal 2027 (+9%), about $3 billion of operating-expense cuts by fiscal 2029, reiterated double-digit adjusted EPS growth in fiscal 2027, and is targeting about $9 billion of buybacks — though fiscal 2026 included a 53rd week that added about $1.6 billion of revenue and about $0.17 of EPS to Q4, flattering the year-on-year comparison. Disney also said it will stop reporting streaming (SVOD) operating income separately from the first quarter of fiscal 2027 — “we do not plan to continue providing this disclosure going forward” — a year after it stopped disclosing Disney+ subscriber counts. Chief financial officer Hugh Johnston told CNBC after the results that Disney expects more layoffs, on top of $392 million of severance booked in fiscal 2026, as part of the roughly $3 billion of run-rate savings targeted by fiscal 2029; Disney+ and Hulu move to a fully unified experience from December 2026. Chief executive Josh D'Amaro said on the November 17 call that an in-house AI rendering tool cuts film-rendering compute needs by about 30%, and called the co-exclusive Netflix licensing deal a “smart bet” (TheStreet, November 19). The Craft Maintenance Council's November 22 ratification deadline passed with the second offer rejected, so the proposal reverts to a strike-authorization vote; no date has been set and talks are expected to resume in December. Disney announced a multiyear strategic partnership with Adobe on November 9, outside this window, to develop customized artificial intelligence tools for its theme parks, cruise operations, film studios and ESPN. Disney will debut “Ozzy Fox,” a children's series animated with AI tools from French startup Animaj, on Disney+ in December, as part of CEO Josh D'Amaro's push to bring AI into animation, finance and film; the same report said a planned $1 billion OpenAI partnership licensing 200+ characters for AI short videos collapsed. The fiscal Q4 quarter had an extra week, expected to add about $600 million to total segment operating income — a benefit that will not recur in fiscal 2027.
