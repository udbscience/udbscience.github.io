DIS — Walt Disney

  • 2026-11-17 — Disney reported fiscal Q4 2026 results before the open: adjusted EPS of $1.77 versus about $1.67 consensus, revenue of $25.9 billion (+16% versus about $25.2 billion expected), and total segment operating income of $5.0 billion (+43%); Experiences revenue was $10.1 billion (+15%, a record quarter and full year) and streaming SVOD operating income doubled to $824 million; the board raised the annual dividend 33% to $2.00 per share (two $1 installments, January 22 and July 13, 2027), guided cash content spending of about $26 billion in fiscal 2027 (+9%), about $3 billion of operating-expense cuts by fiscal 2029, reiterated double-digit adjusted EPS growth in fiscal 2027, and is targeting about $9 billion of buybacks. Why it matters: a clean beat plus a materially higher dividend and buyback target resets the fiscal 2027 earnings and cash-return outlook — though fiscal 2026 included a 53rd week that added about $1.6 billion of revenue and about $0.17 of EPS to Q4, flattering the year-on-year comparison. Reuters
  • 2026-11-17 — Disney said in its fiscal 2026 results that it will stop reporting streaming (SVOD) operating income separately from the first quarter of fiscal 2027 — "we do not plan to continue providing this disclosure going forward" — a year after it stopped disclosing Disney+ subscriber counts, and will instead be judged on overall Entertainment segment operating income. Why it matters: removes a key transparency metric just as streaming profitability was the earnings story, making future streaming performance harder to verify — though Disney will still report SVOD revenue and segment operating income.   • 2026-11-21 — Members of Walt Disney World's Craft Maintenance Council voted down Disney's second contract offer, 231 yes to 735 no, after voting November 20–21; the unions say bargaining continues, with no strike authorized and no park operating change announced — the council's first offer was unanimously voted down on October 13, and its contract expired September 28 and is extended month to month. Why it matters: prolongs labor uncertainty for the Disney World maintenance workforce and keeps strike risk alive into December bargaining, though no walkout is scheduled and the Experiences segment — the earnings driver — is unaffected for now. Southern Regional Council of Carpenters

Chief financial officer Hugh Johnston told CNBC after the results that Disney expects more layoffs, on top of $392 million of severance booked in fiscal 2026, as part of the roughly $3 billion of run-rate savings targeted by fiscal 2029; Disney+ and Hulu move to a fully unified experience from December 2026. Chief executive Josh D'Amaro said on the November 17 call that an in-house AI rendering tool cuts film-rendering compute needs by about 30%, and called the co-exclusive Netflix licensing deal a “smart bet” (TheStreet, November 19). The Carpenters union filed an unfair labor practice claim on November 12 alleging intimidation of workers over strike talk. The Craft Maintenance Council's November 22 ratification deadline passed with the second offer rejected, so the proposal reverts to a strike-authorization vote; no date has been set and talks are expected to resume in December. Disney announced a multiyear strategic partnership with Adobe on November 9, outside this window, to develop customized artificial intelligence tools for its theme parks, cruise operations, film studios and ESPN. Disney will debut “Ozzy Fox,” a children's series animated with AI tools from French startup Animaj, on Disney+ in December, as part of CEO Josh D'Amaro's push to bring AI into animation, finance and film; the same report said a planned $1 billion OpenAI partnership licensing 200+ characters for AI short videos collapsed. The fiscal Q4 quarter had an extra week, expected to add about $600 million to total segment operating income — a benefit that will not recur in fiscal 2027.
