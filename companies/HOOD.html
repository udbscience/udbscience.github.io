HOOD — Robinhood Markets

  • 2026-11-09 — Robinhood sued Missouri's attorney general and gaming commission in federal court, arguing the Commodity Exchange Act preempts Missouri law as applied to sports-related event contracts, after a California court curbed Kalshi's sports contracts on tribal lands and Arkansas sued Kalshi; Robinhood routes its customers' sports event-contract trades to Kalshi, Rothera and Nadex. Why it matters: the outcome decides whether Robinhood can keep offering sports event contracts nationwide — a growth line now facing state-by-state legal challenges — though the filing is only the opening of the case and the court has not ruled. RG.org
  • 2026-11-12 — Robinhood reported October 2026 operating data: net deposits of $15.1 billion (46% annualized growth rate), funded customers of 28.9 million (+130,000 month over month), total platform assets of $383 billion (−3% month over month, +12% year over year); equity volumes of $325 billion (+2% year over year), crypto volumes of $17.0 billion (−48% year over year), event contracts of 5.9 billion (+136% year over year), and margin balances of $22.9 billion (+39% year over year). Why it matters: strong deposit growth and a fast-growing event-contracts line offset a sharp crypto-volume slump, a mixed read on transaction-revenue momentum. GlobeNewswire
  • 2026-11-12 — Louisiana's attorney general sued Robinhood and nine other prediction-market operators (including Kalshi, Polymarket, Coinbase and Gemini), alleging sports event contracts violate state gambling law, and a Tangipahoa Parish judge signed a 10-day ex parte order pausing their Louisiana business, with a hearing on November 30; Louisiana is the 11th state to sue. Why it matters: escalating state-level legal risk to Robinhood's fastest-growing business — event contracts, 5.9 billion traded in October, up 136% year over year — though the order is temporary and the case is only beginning. The Block via TradingView

A California court issued an enforceable injunction on November 9 curbing Kalshi's sports-related event contracts (limited to tribal lands), and Arkansas sued Kalshi the same week. An FM Intelligence analysis of Robinhood's Q3 figures found event-contract revenue per contract fell about 8% quarter over quarter to roughly 1.05 cents while volumes rose about 21% to 16.4 billion contracts; event-contract revenue of $173 million surpassed crypto revenue of $120 million, and the analysis forecast Q4 event-contract revenue of $156–$198 million. Robinhood launched its IPO Access service in the United Kingdom on November 9, letting UK retail investors request shares in eligible US IPOs at the offer price. Robinhood confirmed November 9 it will not object to tokenized HOOD shares trading on the planned OKX–ICE venue, one of only a few issuers not to object. The CFTC issued an impartial-access interpretation on November 5 telling exchanges they may not block customers by state, which Robinhood cited in its Missouri suit.
