The November 16 Wall Street Journal report that Kalshi plans to launch stock and ETF trading within two months is now outside this window. Robinhood reported October 2026 operating data on November 12, just outside this window: net deposits of $15.1 billion (46% annualized growth rate), funded customers of 28.9 million (+130,000 month over month), total platform assets of $383 billion (−3% month over month, +12% year over year); equity volumes of $325 billion (+2% year over year), crypto volumes of $17.0 billion (−48% year over year), event contracts of 5.9 billion (+136% year over year), and margin balances of $22.9 billion (+39% year over year). Robinhood repurchased more than $100 million of its shares in the fourth quarter through November 18, at an average of about $79 — more than four times the $25 million spent in Q3, when the average was about $105 — with more than $1.24 billion of authorization remaining and $5.8 billion of cash at September 30 (Zacks, November 23). Robinhood launched near-real-time IPO pricing tools on November 19, timed to Anthropic's debut. Louisiana's attorney general sued Robinhood and nine other prediction-market operators on November 12, alleging sports event contracts violate state gambling law, and a Tangipahoa Parish judge signed a 10-day ex parte order pausing their Louisiana business; on November 13 the CFTC and the Department of Justice sued Louisiana in federal court to stop state action against prediction markets, and on November 14 Robinhood moved in federal court (E.D. Louisiana) to vacate the order, with its Rothera Exchange joint venture moving to dissolve or modify it. Five Kalshi entities moved to intervene as plaintiffs in the US/CFTC suit against Louisiana on November 18 and join the government's injunction motion, a day after KalshiEX dropped its own federal suit against the state; Judge Africk, who consolidated the cases on November 16, set a status conference for November 20, and on November 20 dismissed all pending preliminary-injunction motions without prejudice, granted all motions to intervene, and left the Louisiana order in place — the order then expired on November 22 with no extension reported. Rothera's and Robinhood's motions to dissolve or modify the Louisiana order carry a December 2 submission date. Separately, New York's case against Kalshi was remanded to state court on November 19; the CFTC's emergency bid to block enforcement was denied by Judge Schofield on November 20 (its second such loss, after Judge Rakoff in July), and Justice Melissa Crane denied the NY AG's temporary restraining order on November 23, setting a preliminary-injunction hearing for January 7 or 8, 2027 and asking both sides to report by November 30 on safeguards for users aged 18 to 20. Nevada's response to Robinhood's Supreme Court petition (No. 26-338, seeking to block a revived investor class action over its 2021 IPO registration statement) is due December 7. Chief executive Vlad Tenev said on X on November 18 that Robinhood will not list “mention-market” contracts (little hedging value, manipulation risk) and called prediction markets its fastest-growing business. Activist group As You Sow filed a resolution on November 20 for Robinhood's next annual meeting calling for a review of its proxy-voting platform, asking it to offer clients more ways to vote, including an independent voting service — a non-binding review request with no near-term earnings impact. The US Supreme Court agreed on November 13 to hear Robinhood's appeal seeking to block a revived investor class action alleging its 2021 IPO registration statement omitted interim financial data showing meme-stock and crypto trading was already cooling. Robinhood agreed to halt new sports event contracts in Ohio and Michigan, reported November 11 (the underlying agreements date to October 26 and September 4 and were disclosed in the October 28 10-Q), trading the halt for the states' promise not to enforce gaming laws while appeals run — the state-by-state shrinkage of the sports-contracts market continues. Robinhood sued Missouri's attorney general and gaming commission in federal court on November 9, arguing the Commodity Exchange Act preempts Missouri law as applied to sports-related event contracts. A California court issued an enforceable injunction on November 9 curbing Kalshi's sports-related event contracts (limited to tribal lands), and Arkansas sued Kalshi the same week. An FM Intelligence analysis of Robinhood's Q3 figures found event-contract revenue per contract fell about 8% quarter over quarter to roughly 1.05 cents while volumes rose about 21% to 16.4 billion contracts; event-contract revenue of $173 million surpassed crypto revenue of $120 million, and the analysis forecast Q4 event-contract revenue of $156–$198 million. Robinhood launched its IPO Access service in the United Kingdom on November 9, letting UK retail investors request shares in eligible US IPOs at the offer price. Robinhood confirmed November 9 it will not object to tokenized HOOD shares trading on the planned OKX–ICE venue, one of only a few issuers not to object. The CFTC issued an impartial-access interpretation on November 5 telling exchanges they may not block customers by state, which Robinhood cited in its Missouri suit.
