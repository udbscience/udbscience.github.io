HOOD — Robinhood Markets

  • 2026-11-12 — Robinhood reported October 2026 operating data: net deposits of $15.1 billion (46% annualized growth rate), funded customers of 28.9 million (+130,000 month over month), total platform assets of $383 billion (−3% month over month, +12% year over year); equity volumes of $325 billion (+2% year over year), crypto volumes of $17.0 billion (−48% year over year), event contracts of 5.9 billion (+136% year over year), and margin balances of $22.9 billion (+39% year over year). Why it matters: strong deposit growth and a fast-growing event-contracts line offset a sharp crypto-volume slump, a mixed read on transaction-revenue momentum. GlobeNewswire
  • 2026-11-12 — Louisiana's attorney general sued Robinhood and nine other prediction-market operators (including Kalshi, Polymarket, Coinbase and Gemini), alleging sports event contracts violate state gambling law, and a Tangipahoa Parish judge signed a 10-day ex parte order pausing their Louisiana business, with a hearing on November 30; Louisiana is the 11th state to sue. On November 13 the CFTC and the Department of Justice sued Louisiana in federal court to stop state action against prediction markets, arguing the contracts are federally regulated swaps. On November 14 Robinhood moved in federal court (E.D. Louisiana) to vacate the order, and its Rothera Exchange joint venture moved to dissolve or modify it and sought an expedited hearing as early as November 16; the order expires November 22 unless extended. Why it matters: escalating state-level legal risk to Robinhood's fastest-growing business — event contracts, 5.9 billion traded in October, up 136% year over year — now set against a federal preemption fight that will decide whether the product can keep expanding. Legal Sports Report iGaming Times (Nov 16, docket-based)
  • 2026-11-16 — The Wall Street Journal reported that Kalshi plans to launch stock and ETF trading within two months. Why it matters: Kalshi is Robinhood's prediction-markets partner; a move into equities would make it a direct competitor in Robinhood's core brokerage business. Seeking Alpha (reporting The Wall Street Journal)

The US Supreme Court agreed on November 13 to hear Robinhood's appeal seeking to block a revived investor class action alleging its 2021 IPO registration statement omitted interim financial data showing meme-stock and crypto trading was already cooling. Robinhood agreed to halt new sports event contracts in Ohio and Michigan, reported November 11 (the underlying agreements date to October 26 and September 4 and were disclosed in the October 28 10-Q), trading the halt for the states' promise not to enforce gaming laws while appeals run — the state-by-state shrinkage of the sports-contracts market continues. Robinhood sued Missouri's attorney general and gaming commission in federal court on November 9, arguing the Commodity Exchange Act preempts Missouri law as applied to sports-related event contracts. A California court issued an enforceable injunction on November 9 curbing Kalshi's sports-related event contracts (limited to tribal lands), and Arkansas sued Kalshi the same week. An FM Intelligence analysis of Robinhood's Q3 figures found event-contract revenue per contract fell about 8% quarter over quarter to roughly 1.05 cents while volumes rose about 21% to 16.4 billion contracts; event-contract revenue of $173 million surpassed crypto revenue of $120 million, and the analysis forecast Q4 event-contract revenue of $156–$198 million. Robinhood launched its IPO Access service in the United Kingdom on November 9, letting UK retail investors request shares in eligible US IPOs at the offer price. Robinhood confirmed November 9 it will not object to tokenized HOOD shares trading on the planned OKX–ICE venue, one of only a few issuers not to object. The CFTC issued an impartial-access interpretation on November 5 telling exchanges they may not block customers by state, which Robinhood cited in its Missouri suit.
