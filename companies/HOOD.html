HOOD — Robinhood Markets

  • 2026-11-19 — The US Senate unanimously passed the DEATH BETS Act (S.4035, Schiff/Curtis) by voice vote, the first prediction-market bill to pass the Senate this Congress; it would amend the Commodity Exchange Act to bar CFTC-registered exchanges from listing or clearing contracts tied to terrorism, assassination, war, US military operations or an individual's death. It is not yet law — the House and the president still need to act — and it expressly does not cover sports contracts. Why it matters: the first federal prediction-market statute to clear a chamber narrows the contract types Robinhood's hub can offer, though sports contracts — the revenue driver and litigation flashpoint — are untouched, while the sponsors' broader bill would bar sports contracts. Office of Sen. Adam Schiff CoinDesk, Nov 20
  • 2026-11-22 — Louisiana's 10-day temporary restraining order barring Robinhood, Kalshi, Polymarket and others from offering event contracts in the state reached its November 22 expiration (3:48 p.m. CT) with no extension reported, after Judge Africk on November 20 dismissed the pending motions to lift it without ruling on them and set new preliminary-injunction briefing targeted for early 2027. Why it matters: lifts the immediate Louisiana ban on Robinhood's event contracts there, though the underlying litigation continues — Louisiana must say by November 24 whether it pursues remand to state court, and the state preliminary-injunction hearing set for November 30 can only go ahead if the case is sent back first. Louisiana Trade & Commerce CoinStats
  • 2026-11-23 — New York County Supreme Court Justice Melissa Crane denied Attorney General Letitia James's request for a temporary restraining order against Kalshi, saying granting it would amount to the “ultimate relief” before both sides had fully briefed the case; Kalshi keeps offering event contracts in New York while the case is argued, with a preliminary-injunction hearing set for January 7 or 8, 2027, and the parties due back November 30 to report on safeguards for users aged 18 to 20. Why it matters: removes the immediate threat of a court order shutting the exchange Robinhood's prediction-markets hub routes through — the fastest-growing part of its business — though the January preliminary-injunction hearing can still curb it, and the judge flagged age safeguards as a live condition. New November 24: Utah's attorney general moved (filed November 23, announced November 24) to dismiss the 14 criminal charges against KalshiEX and co-founders Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara without prejudice, after Kalshi geofenced Utah and halted operations there; a judge must still grant the motion, and Utah's civil case (seeking about $61 million) continues (Utah AG press release, November 24). Also November 24: Ingham County Circuit Court Judge Rosemarie Aquilina held the show-cause hearing on Michigan Attorney General Dana Nessel's request to hold Kalshi in contempt over sports-wagering advertising, but made no ruling, saying she was “very, very concerned” by what she called extreme and targeted advertising; Nessel seeks $7,500 per count for dozens of instances (Bloomberg Law / RG, November 24) — no fine has been imposed; Robinhood had already exited Michigan sports contracts (positions closed by October 9). Louisiana's position on whether it will pursue remand of its enforcement case to state court was due November 24 and had still not been reported as of Thursday, November 26; the next step is a federal Zoom status conference on December 1 at 9 a.m. CST (Judge Africk), and the November 30 state preliminary-injunction hearing is no longer the next step, since the case is in federal court. The November 16 Wall Street Journal report that Kalshi plans to launch stock and ETF trading within two months is now outside this window. Robinhood reported October 2026 operating data on November 12, just outside this window: net deposits of $15.1 billion (46% annualized growth rate), funded customers of 28.9 million (+130,000 month over month), total platform assets of $383 billion (−3% month over month, +12% year over year); equity volumes of $325 billion (+2% year over year), crypto volumes of $17.0 billion (−48% year over year), event contracts of 5.9 billion (+136% year over year), and margin balances of $22.9 billion (+39% year over year). Robinhood repurchased more than $100 million of its shares in the fourth quarter through November 18, at an average of about $79 — more than four times the $25 million spent in Q3, when the average was about $105 — with more than $1.24 billion of authorization remaining and $5.8 billion of cash at September 30 (Zacks, November 23). Robinhood launched near-real-time IPO pricing tools on November 19, timed to Anthropic's debut. Louisiana's attorney general sued Robinhood and nine other prediction-market operators on November 12, alleging sports event contracts violate state gambling law, and a Tangipahoa Parish judge signed a 10-day ex parte order pausing their Louisiana business; on November 13 the CFTC and the Department of Justice sued Louisiana in federal court to stop state action against prediction markets, and on November 14 Robinhood moved in federal court (E.D. Louisiana) to vacate the order, with its Rothera Exchange joint venture moving to dissolve or modify it. Five Kalshi entities moved to intervene as plaintiffs in the US/CFTC suit against Louisiana on November 18 and join the government's injunction motion, a day after KalshiEX dropped its own federal suit against the state; Judge Africk, who consolidated the cases on November 16, set a status conference for November 20, and on November 20 dismissed all pending preliminary-injunction motions without prejudice, granted all motions to intervene, and left the Louisiana order in place — the order then expired on November 22 with no extension reported. Rothera's and Robinhood's motions to dissolve or modify the Louisiana order carry a December 2 submission date. Separately, New York's case against Kalshi was remanded to state court on November 19; the CFTC's emergency bid to block enforcement was denied by Judge Schofield on November 20 (its second such loss, after Judge Rakoff in July), and Justice Melissa Crane denied the NY AG's temporary restraining order on November 23, setting a preliminary-injunction hearing for January 7 or 8, 2027 and asking both sides to report by November 30 on safeguards for users aged 18 to 20. Nevada's response to Robinhood's Supreme Court petition (No. 26-338, seeking to block a revived investor class action over its 2021 IPO registration statement) is due December 7. Chief executive Vlad Tenev said on X on November 18 that Robinhood will not list “mention-market” contracts (little hedging value, manipulation risk) and called prediction markets its fastest-growing business. Activist group As You Sow filed a resolution on November 20 for Robinhood's next annual meeting calling for a review of its proxy-voting platform, asking it to offer clients more ways to vote, including an independent voting service — a non-binding review request with no near-term earnings impact. The US Supreme Court agreed on November 13 to hear Robinhood's appeal seeking to block a revived investor class action alleging its 2021 IPO registration statement omitted interim financial data showing meme-stock and crypto trading was already cooling. Robinhood agreed to halt new sports event contracts in Ohio and Michigan, reported November 11 (the underlying agreements date to October 26 and September 4 and were disclosed in the October 28 10-Q), trading the halt for the states' promise not to enforce gaming laws while appeals run — the state-by-state shrinkage of the sports-contracts market continues. Robinhood sued Missouri's attorney general and gaming commission in federal court on November 9, arguing the Commodity Exchange Act preempts Missouri law as applied to sports-related event contracts. On November 20, Senior District Judge Catherine D. Perry (E.D. Mo.) ordered coordinated pretrial handling of the four Missouri prediction-market suits — Kalshi, Polymarket US, Robinhood Derivatives and Novig — against Missouri Attorney General Catherine Hanaway, transferring the first three to her docket while each case remains separately pending; a telephone conference was set for November 24 to set the preliminary-injunction schedule, with no outcome reported as of November 26 (RG). A California court issued an enforceable injunction on November 9 curbing Kalshi's sports-related event contracts (limited to tribal lands), and Arkansas sued Kalshi the same week. An FM Intelligence analysis of Robinhood's Q3 figures found event-contract revenue per contract fell about 8% quarter over quarter to roughly 1.05 cents while volumes rose about 21% to 16.4 billion contracts; event-contract revenue of $173 million surpassed crypto revenue of $120 million, and the analysis forecast Q4 event-contract revenue of $156–$198 million. Robinhood launched its IPO Access service in the United Kingdom on November 9, letting UK retail investors request shares in eligible US IPOs at the offer price. Robinhood confirmed November 9 it will not object to tokenized HOOD shares trading on the planned OKX–ICE venue, one of only a few issuers not to object. The CFTC issued an impartial-access interpretation on November 5 telling exchanges they may not block customers by state, which Robinhood cited in its Missouri suit. The CFTC has told Kalshi and Polymarket they must stop geofencing US states or explain why by December 5, 2026 (Gaming America).
