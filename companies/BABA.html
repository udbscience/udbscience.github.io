BABA — Alibaba

  • 2026-11-23 — The US Commerce Department added Alibaba's Singapore subsidiary Apex Enterprise Solutions and six Megaspeed-linked entities to its Entity List trade blacklist, effective November 25, requiring US licences — reviewed under a presumption of denial — for American technology; Apex had prepaid about $4.2 billion to Megaspeed and other companies to rent computing capacity in Southeast Asia, and Alibaba called the listing erroneous, saying Apex never diverted advanced computing chips subject to US export restrictions, and that it will pursue legal avenues. Why it matters: puts a US export-control cloud directly over Alibaba's Singapore cloud capacity and the Megaspeed relationship behind last week's lawsuit — the same exposure that drove the roughly 14% ADS slide — and threatens the Southeast Asian compute Alibaba rents for its cloud growth. Reuters, via SRN News Invezz
  • 2026-11-24 — Alibaba and three affiliates sold 25 million ZTO Express shares at $19.28 each, raising about $482 million and cutting its stake to 2.9% from 6.1%, its second major courier-stake sale in two months. Why it matters: a deliberate divestment of a non-core logistics holding that frees cash for the AI/cloud buildout — the funding question at the centre of the valuation after Q3 capex jumped about 60% and free cash flow was a RMB 34.5 billion outflow. SeekingAlpha, via TradingView

Alibaba reported September-quarter results on November 19 — now just outside this window — revenue RMB 271.5 billion (+10%), AI cloud revenue up 53%, non-GAAP net income RMB 19.9 billion, free cash outflow RMB 34.5 billion; on the call chief executive Eddie Wu said most new AI chip supply after 2027 should be domestic T-Head chips. Shareholders sued Alibaba in Manhattan federal court on November 19 (Bihan v. Alibaba, No. 26-cv-10158), alleging it concealed its business relationship with Alice Huang and Megaspeed — suspected of helping China obtain restricted Nvidia chips — and understated export-control risks in its fiscal 2024–2026 annual reports; the suit seeks damages for ADS holders from May 23, 2024 to November 11, 2026, with a lead-plaintiff deadline of January 19, 2027, and names chief executive Eddie Wu as a defendant. Further firms (Kaplan Fox on November 21, Robbins on November 23, Bleichmar Fonti & Auld on November 24) have since solicited plaintiffs for the same suit. Kahn Swick & Foti and Levi & Korsinsky also issued notices on November 24. Bronstein Gewirtz & Grossman issued a further notice on November 26. Bloomberg reported November 25 that Alibaba has held early talks with banks about an offshore bond sale in 2027 (offshore yuan and US dollars) to fund AI expansion, seeking a National Development and Reform Commission issuance quota — explicitly preliminary, with size, timing and currency undecided and no company confirmation; no confirmation had come as of November 28. November 24 coverage of the Entity List action (Wall Street Journal, Reuters, IBTimes) was re-reporting of the November 23 action, adding that Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said authorities will continue examining whether Megaspeed violated local laws, that the Nvidia chips Megaspeed bought never physically entered Singapore but were routed through other countries, that investigating activities outside Singapore presents jurisdictional challenges and US authorities had not provided all the information Singapore requested, and that Singapore investigated former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang but released her after finding insufficient grounds to prosecute; Senators Maria Cantwell (calling for a broader investigation) and Pete Ricketts (demanding an explanation for the delay) also reacted. The designation does not establish that Alibaba or its subsidiary illegally transferred American chips to China; the restrictions apply to the named entities, not Alibaba's entire group. The rule was formally published in the Federal Register on November 25; no new entities were added. Susquehanna cut its Alibaba price target to $145 from $190 on November 24, citing cloud-growth concerns — a lone analyst move, excluded. The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule (November 15) requiring licences for advanced-chip shipments to data centres in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil and curbing remote access to advanced compute by China-headquartered firms; Alibaba fell about 5% in Hong Kong on November 17, and reports note it previously rented Nvidia capacity in Malaysia via Singapore-based Megaspeed International. TrendForce reported November 20, citing The Paper/Nikkei, that Alibaba is pursuing a separate Hong Kong listing for T-Head, reportedly to raise US$2–3 billion in 2027 — unconfirmed; on the call Wu confirmed only that T-Head confidentially filed for a Hong Kong listing, with no timeline. Alibaba released Qwen4-Max on November 16 — a 2.4-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with a 1-million-token context window and native vision, priced below its predecessor — sharpening Alibaba Cloud's AI competitiveness ahead of the results. On the November 19 earnings call, Wu said mass production of the next-generation Zhenwu V900 AI chip (three times the performance of the M890) begins in the first quarter of 2027, with V900 supernode clusters at commercial scale in the second quarter of 2027 and the J900 in 2028; he also said Alibaba's long-term 20 GW data-centre capacity target may be revised downward amid changing global compute policy, while cloud revenue growth is guided at 30%–45% next year and back to 50% or more in 2028, and the calendar-2030 targets ($100 billion external cloud revenue, 20%+ margin) stand. Alibaba disclosed on November 10, outside this window, that the Hong Kong Stock Exchange has cleared it to spin off T-Head, its data-center chip unit, which confidentially filed for a Hong Kong IPO on September 11; no size or post-listing stake was set. The final Singles Day tally put total Double 11 online GMV up 4.8% year over year to RMB 1.78 trillion, sharply slower than 2025's +14.2%, with platform GMV (including Alibaba's Tmall, the largest single platform) up only 3.7% versus 12.3% last year, amid price wars and cautious consumers, according to Syntun's final figures (November 12). The US BIS interim rule takes effect November 19 (Federal Register publication), with data centres serving China-headquartered firms given 90 days, to mid-February 2027, to obtain licences; commercial recommender systems are exempt.
