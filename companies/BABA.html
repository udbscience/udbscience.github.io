BABA — Alibaba

  • 2026-11-19 — Alibaba reported September-quarter (Q2 FY27) results before the open: revenue of RMB 271.52 billion ($40.7 billion), up 10% year over year and ahead of the RMB 269.81 billion LSEG consensus, with AI cloud and compute revenue of RMB 60.97 billion, up 53%, and recurring revenue from external cloud customers up 67%; non-GAAP net income rose 92% to RMB 19.9 billion and reported net income more than tripled on investment gains, but non-GAAP diluted EPS per ADS of RMB 8.10 missed the RMB 11.16 expected, and free cash flow was an outflow of RMB 34.47 billion, wider than the RMB 21.84 billion outflow a year earlier. Why it matters: cloud/AI is now the growth engine, but the EPS miss and widening cash outflow test whether the AI buildout is paying off — the core valuation question for the stock. Reuters
  • 2026-11-19 — On the earnings call, chief executive Eddie Wu said that after 2027 “the vast majority of our new AI chip supply should consist primarily of our own domestic T-Head chips,” that the next-generation Qwen model is already being trained on T-Head chips, and that T-Head production capacity will ramp “very significantly” by 2027 — a pivot to domestic silicon in response to the US export-control rule that took effect the same day. Why it matters: it is the strategic answer to the export-control threat to Alibaba Cloud's growth: whether cloud revenue can recover toward the guided 50%+ growth in 2028 now depends on T-Head chips performing at scale, with T-Head still trailing foreign rivals on performance. TechXplore Reuters Breakingviews, Nov 20
  • 2026-11-19 — Shareholders sued Alibaba in Manhattan federal court (Bihan v. Alibaba, No. 26-cv-10158), alleging it concealed its business relationship with Alice Huang and Megaspeed — suspected of helping China obtain restricted Nvidia chips — and understated export-control risks in its fiscal 2024–2026 annual reports; the suit seeks damages for ADS holders from May 23, 2024 to November 11, 2026, with a lead-plaintiff deadline of January 19, 2027, and names chief executive Eddie Wu as a defendant. Why it matters: adds litigation and regulatory risk directly to the AI/cloud growth story — the same Megaspeed exposure behind the roughly 14% ADS slide over four days last week — and raises the risk Alibaba could lose access to advanced US computing products; the allegations are unadjudicated. Reuters, via WTBX Kahn Swick & Foti notice, via BusinessWire

The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule (November 15) requiring licences for advanced-chip shipments to data centres in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil and curbing remote access to advanced compute by China-headquartered firms; Alibaba fell about 5% in Hong Kong on November 17, and reports note it previously rented Nvidia capacity in Malaysia via Singapore-based Megaspeed International. Kaplan Fox alerted investors on November 21 that a securities class action has been filed — the same Bihan v. Alibaba case above, a second firm joining. TrendForce reported November 20, citing The Paper/Nikkei, that Alibaba is pursuing a separate Hong Kong listing for T-Head, reportedly to raise US$2–3 billion in 2027 — unconfirmed; on the call Wu confirmed only that T-Head confidentially filed for a Hong Kong listing, with no timeline. Alibaba released Qwen4-Max on November 16 — a 2.4-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with a 1-million-token context window and native vision, priced below its predecessor — sharpening Alibaba Cloud's AI competitiveness ahead of the results. On the November 19 earnings call, Wu said mass production of the next-generation Zhenwu V900 AI chip (three times the performance of the M890) begins in the first quarter of 2027, with V900 supernode clusters at commercial scale in the second quarter of 2027 and the J900 in 2028; he also said Alibaba's long-term 20 GW data-centre capacity target may be revised downward amid changing global compute policy, while cloud revenue growth is guided at 30%–45% next year and back to 50% or more in 2028, and the calendar-2030 targets ($100 billion external cloud revenue, 20%+ margin) stand. Alibaba disclosed on November 10, outside this window, that the Hong Kong Stock Exchange has cleared it to spin off T-Head, its data-center chip unit, which confidentially filed for a Hong Kong IPO on September 11; no size or post-listing stake was set. The final Singles Day tally put total Double 11 online GMV up 4.8% year over year to RMB 1.78 trillion, sharply slower than 2025's +14.2%, with platform GMV (including Alibaba's Tmall, the largest single platform) up only 3.7% versus 12.3% last year, amid price wars and cautious consumers, according to Syntun's final figures (November 12). The US BIS interim rule takes effect November 19 (Federal Register publication), with data centres serving China-headquartered firms given 90 days, to mid-February 2027, to obtain licences; commercial recommender systems are exempt.
